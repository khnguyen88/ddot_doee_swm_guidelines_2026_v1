# 06 – BMP Specifications: 3.4 Impervious Surface Disconnection

**Source:** `docs/doee presentation/06_BMP Specifications_3.4 Impervious Surface Disconnection.pptx`

## Overview
Impervious surface disconnection redirects runoff from impervious surfaces onto pervious receiving areas, effectively converting a runoff source into a BMP. It is difficult to apply in ultra-urban situations but uses green space very efficiently when feasible.

## Three Disconnection Options

| Option | Description |
|---|---|
| **D-1** | Simple Disconnection to Pervious Area (compacted cover — turf/landscaping) |
| **D-2** | Simple Disconnection to Conservation Area (natural or minimally disturbed area, excluding regulated wetlands and buffers) |
| **D-3** | Simple Disconnection to Compost Amended Filter Path (engineered soil amendment path) |

## Design Criteria

| Parameter | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Rooftop CDA | ≤ 1,000 SF per disconnection point |
| Non-rooftop flow path | Longest contributing impervious flow path ≤ 75 ft |
| Receiving area width | 10 ft minimum – 25 ft maximum |
| Receiving area length | 15 ft minimum – 100 ft maximum |
| Wider receiving areas | Allowed with sheet flow or level spreader |

## Retention Value Calculations

| Disconnection Type | Soil Type | Retention Value |
|---|---|---|
| D-1 — Compacted Cover | A/B soils | 4.0 CF per 100 SF of receiving area (42% volume reduction) |
| D-1 — Compacted Cover | C/D soils | 2.0 CF per 100 SF of receiving area (21% volume reduction) |
| D-2 — Conservation Area | Any | 6.0 CF per 100 SF of receiving conservation area (63% volume reduction) |
| D-3 — Compost Amended Filter Path | Any | 4.0 CF per 100 SF of receiving area (42% volume reduction) |

## Key Considerations
- Requires careful site planning — works best where green space is available adjacent to impervious surfaces
- Conservation area must not include regulated wetlands or buffer areas (D-2)
- Higher soil hydraulic conductivity (A/B soils) yields better retention credit than C/D soils (D-1)
