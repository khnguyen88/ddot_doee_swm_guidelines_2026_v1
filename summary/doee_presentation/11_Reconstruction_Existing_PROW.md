# 11 – Reconstruction of Existing PROW (MEP)

**Source:** `docs/doee presentation/11_Reconstruction of Existing PROW.pptx`

## Overview
When a major regulated project disturbs adjacent public right-of-way (PROW), stormwater retention requirements apply to that PROW disturbance — but compliance follows a Maximum Extent Practicable (MEP) process rather than a hard numerical target.

## When PROW MEP Applies

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Type 1** | Federal or municipal roads, alleys, sidewalks, trails |
| **Type 2** | Private development adjacent sidewalks and alleys |

## When PROW MEP Does NOT Apply
- Major regulated project that does not disturb adjacent PROW
- Voluntary retrofits of existing PROW
- PROW disturbance limited to: trenches, driveways, utilities, aprons, or minor disturbance
- Disturbance of **new** PROW (not previously PROW)

## Principles of PROW MEP
1. Maximize BMP placement and sizing
2. Innovate — integrate green with grey infrastructure
3. Minimize impervious widths
4. Maximize land cover types with low runoff (compacted and natural cover)
5. Maximize tree canopy — plant/preserve trees, amend soils, increase soil volumes, connect tree roots to stormwater runoff
6. Use impervious surface disconnection (e.g., drain sidewalk to continuous tree planting strip)
7. Manage co-mingled stormwater — prioritize roadway runoff conveyance and control
8. Over-control roadway runoff beyond LOD to compensate for less retention elsewhere
9. Use porous pavement/pavers for low-traffic roads, parking, shoulders, sidewalks
10. Integrate BMPs into traffic calming measures

## Parcel PROW MEP Steps
1. Calculate SWRv for the PROW disturbance area
2. Prioritize managing roadway runoff inside the curb line
3. Place, size, and design PROW BMPs to maximize retention (Guidebook Ch. 3 specs, Appendix B priorities, DDOT LID Standards)

## Design Example

**Scenario:** Corner property with 200 ft × 10 ft PROW disturbance (sidewalk)

```
SWRv = 1.2 in × (0.95 × 100%) × 2,000 SF × 7.48/12 = 1,421 gallons
```
Site has poor infiltration; underdrain connections available.

### Constraints Identified
- ADA crossing requirements (no BMP placement)
- Driveways (no BMP)
- Bus stop (no BMP)
- Building exit + 5-ft-wide sidewalk minimum (limits width)
- Utilities (underground conflict)
- Existing trees (preserved but can't place pavement there)

### BMP Placement
- **3 existing trees preserved** (large trees, 20 ft³ each)
- **220 SF bioretention area** with shallow ponding → 823 gallons storage × 0.6 = 494 gallons

### Result
| | Gallons |
|---|---|
| Tree retention (3 × 20 ft³ × 7.48) | 449 |
| Bioretention (494 gallons) | 494 |
| **SWRv Achieved** | **943** |
| **SWRv Required** | **1,421** |

Required SWRv **not fully met**, but the MEP process was properly followed — this is an acceptable outcome.

### Recalculated SWRv for Reduced Impervious Cover
After accounting for permeable surface changes (16% of PROW now compacted cover):
```
SWRv = 1.2 in × (0.95 × 84% + 0.25 × 16%) × 2,000 SF × 7.48/12 = 1,254 gallons
```
Even with the reduced SWRv target, MEP achieved 943/1,254 gallons — demonstrating maximum practicable effort.
