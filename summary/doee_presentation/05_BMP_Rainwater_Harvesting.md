# 05 – BMP Specifications: 3.3 Rainwater Harvesting

**Source:** `docs/doee presentation/05_BMP Specifications_3.3 Rainwater Harvesting.pptx`

## Overview
Rainwater harvesting captures roof or site runoff in a storage tank and reuses it for a dedicated demand (irrigation, toilet flushing, cooling towers, etc.). Retention credit depends on tank size and the reliability of that demand.

## Feasibility Criteria
- Tank must be sized appropriately for the site's dedicated demand
- A consistent, ongoing dedicated demand is required (irrigation, interior reuse, etc.)
- Minimal space or setback requirements make it adaptable to constrained sites
- Public health risks managed through a risk-based framework — see **Appendix N** (Rainwater Harvesting Treatment and Management Requirements)

## Conveyance & Pretreatment
- Inflow pipe must be positioned higher than the outlet pipe
- Overflow mechanism required
- Pretreatment filters required upstream of tank
- A distribution system (pump or gravity-fed) is required to deliver water to the demand

## Design Criteria – Volumes
The tank must account for four volume zones:
1. **Low water volume** — minimum operating reserve
2. **Detention volume** — active storage between storms
3. **Overflow volume** — temporary surge capacity
4. **Freeboard** — safety buffer at top

## System Sizing Tool
Sizing calculations should be performed using DOEE's online sizing tool:  
`https://doee.dc.gov/swguidebook`

## Landscaping Criteria
- Delineate the area to be irrigated
- Default irrigation demand: **1 inch per week between May and October**
- If additional demand is needed, a Certified Landscape Expert must sign off on the increased demand

## Retention Value
Retention credit is based on the volume reliably consumed (drawn down) before the next storm. DOEE's sizing tool calculates this based on tank size, contributing area, and demand rate.
