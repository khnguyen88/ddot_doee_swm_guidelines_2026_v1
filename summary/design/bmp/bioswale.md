# Bioswale — Design Guidance Summary

**Sources:**
- `docs/2014-0421-DDOT Green Infrastructure Standards.pdf` — Section 33.14.5
- `docs/DOEE Stormwater Management Guidebook January 2020.pdf` — Section 3.9 (Open Channel Systems)

---

## Overview

A bioswale is a drainage channel or linear infiltration basin, typically vegetated with trees, shrubs, groundcovers, or turf, located adjacent to a roadway or sidewalk. Under DDOT's framework it is equivalent to DOEE's Dry Swale/Bio-swale. Bioswales may convey or retain stormwater. Swales that receive direct roadway runoff are designed per the DOEE Guidebook's open channel criteria.

- **DDOT facility type:** Bioswale (subset of DOEE Open Channel Systems)
- **DDOT context:** Public right-of-way, adjacent to roadway or sidewalk
- **Online/offline:** Online (no curb), unless converted to offline design

## Soil Profile

| Layer | Specification |
|-------|--------------|
| Mulch (top) | 3 in triple-shredded hardwood mulch |
| Bioretention Soil (filter media) | 18–36 in; min 24 in where trees are present, 30 in preferred |
| Choker layer | 2–4 in of No. 8 or No. 89 washed stone above underdrain stone |
| Underdrain stone | No. 57 washed stone; 2 in min above and below underdrain pipe |
| Optional infiltration sump | Open-graded No. 57 stone below underdrain invert |

**Filter media composition:** 80–90% sand, 10–20% silt/clay (max 10% clay), 3–5% organic content; hydraulic conductivity 2–6 in/hr.

## Underdrain and Drainage

| Parameter | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Underdrain pipe | 4- or 6-in perforated Sched. 40 PVC (HDPE acceptable for small BMPs) |
| Pipe perforations | 3 or 4 rows of 3/8-in at 6-in o.c. |
| Clean-out spacing | Max 100 ft; required for all underdrains |
| Observation wells (no underdrain) | Max 100 ft spacing; shown on plans |
| Drawdown time | Max 72 hr for 1.2-in retention volume (DDOT); 72 hr max (DOEE) |
| Min depth to seasonal high water table | 2 ft below bottom of facility |

---

## Pretreatment

- Stone-filled forebay, grass filter strip, or stone diaphragm at inlets
- Check dams in sloped bioswales to maintain ponding depth; material options: waterproof membrane, PVC sheeting, acrylic sheeting, concrete

---

## Safety and Edge Conditions

| Condition | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Depths/slopes requiring barriers | NOT allowed for bioswales — design must avoid barriers |
| Bioswale adjacent to roadway (no curb) | 24 in graded shoulder; 3:1 max side slope; max 5 ft drop |
| Pedestrian crossings | 1 paved 6-ft crossing between each tree (high-traffic); every other tree (low-traffic) |
| Plant height between curb and sidewalk | < 18 in above sidewalk level (excluding trees) |

## Liner Requirements

- Within **10 ft of a structure/building**: liner on adjacent side
- Within **5 ft of roadway face of curb**: liner on road side
- **Hot spots** (as defined by DOEE): liner on all sides with subsurface drainage
